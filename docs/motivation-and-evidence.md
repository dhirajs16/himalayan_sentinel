# Motivation and evidence

Checked 19 October 2026. This is a factual reference for planning, not a Devpost submission description.

## Learner's stated motivation

The learner identified recent flash floods in Rasuwa and Gorkha, and a Nepal prime minister's UN speech about timely information and warnings, as the reason for building Himalayan Sentinel. Record this without inventing personal disaster exposure or user research.

## Verified chronology

- **26 August 2026 — Rasuwa and neighbouring districts:** UN Nepal records a major flood surge and response. See the [UN Nepal situation reports](https://nepal.un.org/en/323255-nepal-rasuwa-floods-2026-situation-reports) and [FAO account](https://nepal.un.org/en/323263-after-floods-protecting-food-livelihoods-and-recovery). These establish event context, not a warning lead time for this application.
- **24 September 2026 — UN General Assembly speech:** The Ministry of Foreign Affairs publishes the prime minister's address dated 24 September in New York and posted on 25 September. It discusses the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli disaster and calls for satellite-data sharing, glacial-lake monitoring, stronger early-warning cooperation and cross-border response. Source: [official address](https://mofa.gov.np/content/1890/address-by-the-rt--hon--prime-minister/).
- **11 October 2026 — Gorkha:** UN Nepal records Budhi Gandaki flooding in Gorkha that day and emphasizes preparedness and early-warning investment. Source: [UN Nepal statement](https://nepal.un.org/en/324212-un-nepal-statement-budhi-gandaki-river-flooding). This event followed the September address; it cannot be described as an event covered by that earlier speech.

## Claim verification

The learner recalled a statement that earlier data and alerts could have saved thousands of lives. The official address supports the broader importance of shared information and warning systems, but the exact recalled statement and numerical counterfactual were not verified. Do not attribute that sentence to the prime minister or assign a number of potentially saved lives to Himalayan Sentinel without a specific supporting source and analysis.

Historical GLOF data must retain its own coverage dates. An event mentioned here is not automatically included in the local historical database, nor does it establish that a particular lake, report or model would have predicted the disaster.

## Implications for planning

These are design inferences from the learner's motivation, not conclusions of the cited sources:

- Prioritize a responder's path from relevant evidence to a reviewed notice.
- Show source time, retrieval time, geographic coverage, uncertainty and unavailable feeds.
- Distinguish observed river data, weather forecasts, static inventories and historical incidents.
- Provide readable public notices and English/Nepali safety labels within the demonstrated workflow.
- Verify technical outcomes such as retrieval and publication without treating them as proven emergency-response effectiveness.

The prototype does not establish official adoption, validated prediction, guaranteed communication reach or prevented casualties.
